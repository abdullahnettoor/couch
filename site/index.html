The Remote

Your phone is the remote.

Scan the QR code on the Screen once. After that your phone is a D-pad, volume and Now Playing, and every press moves the highlight on the TV. Pair every phone in the household; guests can pair one too.

  • No app to install on the phone. The Remote is a web page.
  • Mac keyboard and trackpad work too, for when you are at the Mac.

Apps

The Apps you already use.

YouTube opens as its own TV experience, and Netflix opens on Netflix's own website. You use your own accounts. Couch gives no access to anything you could not open in a browser.

  • YouTube looks and behaves like the YouTube app on a smart TV. It shows a code on the Screen; enter it at youtube.com/activate on your phone to sign in.
  • Netflix is driven from the Remote with a clear focus ring, and signs in on its own page on the Screen.
  • More Apps can join the list later.

The Library

A folder of videos becomes a shelf.

Point Couch at the folders where your videos live. They appear as a tidy shelf with a picture for each video, a cleaned-up title and where you stopped. Press play and it resumes from there.

  • Plays through its own built-in player, with subtitles and volume from the Remote.
  • Continue watching sits on Home, one press away.
  • Film and show details come from TMDB and are optional. Turn them off and the Library uses frames from your videos.

Calm by design

No account, no cloud, no tracking.

  • No Couch accountThere is nothing to sign up for, and no profiles. The household shares one Screen.
  • No cloud of its ownYour Library, your place in each video and your settings stay on the Mac.
  • No trackingNo Couch analytics. This site uses no cookies. Services you open in Couch keep their own sign-ins and cookies, under their own privacy policies.
  • Open sourceRead every line, or build it yourself, under GPL-3.0-or-later.

What Couch connects to

  • The services you open. YouTube and Netflix load straight from their own sites, like in a browser, and so do the pictures for Continue watching entries.
  • Google's Widevine component. Downloaded once, for video that needs DRM. Couch does not ship it.
  • TMDB, for film and show details. It searches titles and fetches posters and stills. It is on when a TMDB key is set (a release build can carry one, or you can add your own), and you can turn it off in Settings.
  • Your phone, over your home Wi-Fi. The Remote page comes from the Mac on your local network, not from the internet.

Couch does not check for updates or send anything to a Couch server.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is it free?

Yes. Couch is free and open source. On Gumroad you choose what to pay, including nothing, and you can also download it from GitHub Releases at no cost.

Do I need accounts?

Not for Couch: there is no Couch account and no profiles. YouTube and Netflix use your own accounts. YouTube shows a code on the Screen; enter it at youtube.com/activate on your phone. Netflix signs in on its own page on the Screen.

Does it work with Netflix?

Couch shows Netflix's own website inside the Screen and lets you drive it from the Remote. You need your own Netflix account, and playback follows what Netflix's website allows on a Mac.

Why does macOS warn me?

Couch is an early release and Apple has not notarised it yet. Open Couch once. When macOS says it can't check it, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and choose Open Anyway. On macOS 15 and later, right-click → Open no longer works there. You only do this once.

Is it open source?

Yes, under GPL-3.0-or-later. The code is on GitHub.

What about Windows or Linux?

Not for now. Couch is built for a Mac on Apple silicon running macOS 14 or later. Any phone with a browser can be the Remote; it is tested on Android.
